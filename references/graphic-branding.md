# Graphic Design & Branding

## Scope

Create clear, distinctive, production-ready visual communication and identity systems across logos, brand assets, posters, social content, packaging, editorial layouts, presentations, infographics, and print/digital collateral.

Creative direction owns the overall concept. This reference governs graphic execution and brand-system design.

## 1. Brief and requirements

Confirm:
- Communication objective, audience, message, and call to action.
- Brand name, positioning, personality, and existing identity assets.
- Required text, language, spelling, data, and legal notices.
- Format, dimensions, orientation, resolution, color mode, and delivery platform.
- Print specifications or digital display constraints.
- Required editable/source files and export formats.
- Reference assets, license conditions, deadline, and approval owner.

Identify mandatory content and protect it from accidental omission or alteration.

## 2. Visual hierarchy and composition

Establish a clear reading order. Use scale, position, spacing, contrast, alignment, grouping, and typography to guide attention. Ensure the main message is immediately understandable at the intended viewing size.

Use grids and consistent spacing systems where useful. Balance visual weight, negative space, rhythm, and focal points. Avoid overcrowding, arbitrary decoration, weak alignment, and competing calls to action.

Design for actual viewing conditions: phone screens, thumbnails, presentation displays, printed pages, packaging shelves, or signage distances.

## 3. Color and typography

Choose color for meaning, brand consistency, legibility, reproduction, and accessibility. Check foreground/background contrast and avoid relying on color alone to communicate status or meaning.

Choose typefaces appropriate to language, audience, tone, and platform. Establish a controlled typographic hierarchy for titles, subtitles, body text, labels, and captions. Check font licensing, glyph coverage, diacritics, punctuation, and rendering in the target environment.

Do not use too many type families or styles without a clear system.

## 4. Brand identity systems

For identity work, define:
- Logo construction and approved variants.
- Clear space, minimum size, and background behavior.
- Color palette and usage proportions.
- Typography and hierarchy.
- Supporting shapes, patterns, iconography, and imagery.
- Layout and composition principles.
- Tone of voice where relevant.
- Incorrect-use examples and constraints.

Preserve logo proportions and recognizable features. Do not redraw or approximate a supplied logo when the original asset is available and should be used. Do not distort, recolor, crop, or add effects contrary to the approved identity specification.

A brand system should be usable consistently by other people, not just look convincing in one showcase mockup.

## 5. Posters and social graphics

Prioritize the message and audience action. Ensure the title, key information, supporting details, and source/attribution are readable at realistic display sizes. Design platform-specific crops and safe areas when required; do not assume one export fits every platform.

For multi-slide or recurring content, maintain consistent typography, margins, color behavior, and navigation cues while allowing enough variation to sustain attention.

## 6. Packaging and editorial work

For packaging, verify dielines, folds, bleed, safe areas, barcode and regulatory requirements, material behavior, print process, and physical viewing conditions. Mockups are presentation aids, not substitutes for production-ready artwork.

For editorial layouts, establish page grids, baseline and spacing rhythm, running elements, image captions, folios, and consistent heading levels. Check page order, trim, bleed, margins, and embedded fonts before delivery.

## 7. Infographics and data graphics

Preserve the accuracy and context of the source data. Select chart forms that communicate comparisons and relationships honestly. Label units, scales, sources, dates, and uncertainty where applicable. Do not distort axes, omit material context, or use decorative complexity that obscures the information.

## 8. Production workflow

1. Validate brief, content, assets, and specifications.
2. Establish visual direction and hierarchy.
3. Create a layout or rough composition.
4. Resolve typography, color, imagery, and details.
5. Review at actual output size and across relevant backgrounds/devices.
6. Prepare editable master files and final exports.
7. Inspect exported files and record version/source information.

Use appropriate formats:
- SVG or other vector formats for scalable vector artwork where supported.
- PDF for print-ready or fixed-layout delivery when properly prepared.
- PNG for lossless raster graphics and transparency.
- JPEG for photographic imagery where appropriate.
- WebP or other modern formats for supported web delivery.
- Native project files for future editing when requested and available.

Confirm color mode, resolution, bleed, embedded/outlined fonts, transparency, compression, and export settings against the actual destination. Do not promise print readiness without checking printer requirements.

## 9. AI-assisted design and rights

AI may assist with ideation, asset creation, layout exploration, and production, but the final work must be reviewed for text accuracy, visual defects, consistency, originality, and licensing constraints.

Check rights for fonts, stock images, icons, templates, textures, and generated or third-party assets. Keep attribution and license records where required. Do not remove watermarks or present third-party work as original.

## 10. Quality checks

Inspect:
- Exact spelling, punctuation, names, numbers, and required copy.
- Hierarchy, readability, contrast, alignment, spacing, and balance.
- Logo fidelity and brand consistency.
- Image quality, cropping, resolution, and artifacting.
- Font rendering and missing glyphs.
- Platform crops, safe areas, bleed, and export dimensions.
- Transparency, color mode, and file integrity.
- Source/license/attribution requirements.
- Editable file and export consistency.

## Deliverables

Provide the agreed final exports, editable/source files where requested, dimensions and format notes, linked assets or license notes where relevant, and a concise handoff describing intended use and any limitations.

## Definition of done

The design meets the communication brief, all required content is accurate and readable, the brand system is respected, exports match the destination specifications, relevant files are usable, and rights and known limitations are documented.
