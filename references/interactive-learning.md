# Interactive Learning Design

## Purpose and scope

Design digital or physical learning experiences in which learners actively engage with content, practice skills, receive feedback, and demonstrate progress. This reference governs instructional design and educational integrity. Game design governs entertainment mechanics; UI/UX governs general interface usability; development implements the technical experience.

## 1. Learner and context analysis

Identify learner age or level, prior knowledge, language, literacy, abilities, learning environment, device access, session length, facilitator involvement, and constraints. Do not assume all learners in an age band have identical abilities. Consider independent use, classroom use, and shared-device conditions.

## 2. Learning objectives

Write observable objectives describing what learners should know, understand, or do. Use specific verbs and define evidence of achievement. Separate learning objectives from engagement goals such as time spent, stars earned, or levels completed.

Align each activity, question, explanation, and assessment with one or more objectives. Remove interactions that add effort without supporting learning or meaningful motivation.

## 3. Instructional structure

Choose an instructional sequence appropriate to the subject:
- Introduce and activate relevant prior knowledge.
- Explain or model the target concept.
- Provide guided practice.
- Offer independent practice or application.
- Give feedback and opportunities to correct errors.
- Assess achievement and summarize progress.

Use short, coherent steps for younger or novice learners. Manage cognitive load by introducing one meaningful challenge at a time and avoiding unnecessary text, animation, or competing stimuli.

## 4. Content accuracy and source integrity

Verify facts, terminology, examples, and answers against reliable sources appropriate to the domain. Record source title, author or institution, URL or bibliographic detail, access date where useful, and any adaptation.

For religious, medical, legal, or other sensitive subject matter, follow the project's explicit authority and review requirements. Do not invent quotations, attributions, rulings, or source references. Distinguish direct quotation, translation, paraphrase, and explanation. Project-specific standards must be recorded in the project brief rather than assumed universally.

## 5. Interaction design

Select interactions that match the learning objective: retrieval practice, matching, sorting, sequencing, classification, guided exploration, scenario decisions, construction, or reflection. Ensure the input method is clear and suitable for the learner and device.

Define instructions, valid inputs, attempt rules, hints, feedback, retry, skip, completion, and recovery. Do not use interaction novelty as a substitute for meaningful practice. Ensure tasks can be completed without relying on color alone, audio alone, or fine motor precision where avoidable.

## 6. Feedback and assessment

Feedback should be timely, specific, supportive, and instructionally useful. When an answer is incorrect, explain or guide toward the relevant concept rather than merely displaying failure. Avoid shaming language.

Define assessment rules before production:
- What counts as correct or complete?
- Are partial answers accepted?
- Can learners retry?
- Are hints available, and how do they affect scoring?
- What progress is stored?
- What evidence indicates mastery?

Ensure scoring and rewards do not misrepresent actual learning. Distinguish practice completion from demonstrated mastery.

## 7. Motivation and progression

Use meaningful goals, visible progress, appropriate challenge, encouragement, and rewards that reinforce effort or learning. Avoid manipulative pressure, unnecessary timers, punitive loss, or reward structures that encourage random guessing. Make unlock criteria understandable and achievable.

## 8. Age-appropriate content and presentation

Use language, reading load, examples, visuals, pacing, and controls appropriate to the intended learner. Explain unfamiliar terms. Ensure instructions can be understood before a task begins. For young learners, prefer concise directions, consistent control patterns, clear feedback, and opportunities for supported practice.

Consider adult or facilitator needs when the experience is used in a classroom or supervised environment.

## 9. Multimedia and accessibility

Use images, audio, animation, and video to clarify or reinforce learning. Avoid decorative media that competes with essential content. Provide captions, transcripts, text alternatives, volume control, and reduced-motion options where relevant. Ensure visual information has adequate contrast and is not communicated only through color.

## 10. Technical integration

Coordinate content schema, question identifiers, answer validation, randomization, scoring, hints, progression, persistence, analytics, and localization with the development team. Validate all content records before integration. Keep educational content maintainable and traceable to its source.

## 11. Quality assurance

Perform separate reviews for:
- **Subject accuracy:** facts, answers, terminology, sources, translations.
- **Instructional alignment:** objective, activity, feedback, assessment.
- **Learner suitability:** age, language, reading load, cognitive load.
- **Interaction:** input clarity, retry, hints, completion, error handling.
- **Accessibility:** captions, text alternatives, contrast, keyboard/touch alternatives.
- **Technical behavior:** state, scoring, persistence, randomization, responsiveness.
- **Content integrity:** missing items, duplicates, ambiguity, answer-key errors.

Have a qualified subject reviewer assess sensitive or specialist content when required. Do not claim expert review without it.

## Deliverables

Provide learning objectives, learner profile, instructional sequence, content inventory and sources, activity specifications, feedback and assessment rules, accessibility considerations, integration requirements, and QA results.

## Definition of done

The experience supports its stated learning objectives, content is accurate and traceable, interactions and feedback are age- and context-appropriate, assessment rules are clear, accessibility and technical requirements are addressed, and the relevant educational and functional checks are documented.
