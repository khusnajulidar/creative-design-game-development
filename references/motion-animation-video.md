# Motion, Animation & Video

## Purpose and scope

Produce time-based visual and audio experiences including motion graphics, 2D/3D animation, explainers, promotional video, tutorials, title sequences, and in-product animation. Creative direction defines the concept; illustration/3D creates visual assets; this reference governs timing, movement, editing, compositing, and audiovisual delivery.

## 1. Brief and technical requirements

Confirm objective, audience, message, platform, duration, aspect ratio, frame rate, resolution, orientation, language, accessibility needs, audio requirements, distribution, and delivery formats. Identify source assets, licenses, brand rules, and whether editable project files are required.

## 2. Script and storytelling

Define the core message and viewer journey. Structure the piece with a clear opening, development, and conclusion or call to action. Write for the actual duration and viewing context. Avoid excessive information, unsupported claims, and narration that merely repeats on-screen text.

For instructional content, align sequence and pacing with the learning objective. For product or promotional content, make the intended value and next action clear.

## 3. Storyboard and animatic

Plan shots, scenes, transitions, framing, movement, text, voiceover, sound, and timing. Use a storyboard to identify missing information and continuity issues before detailed production. An animatic can validate pacing and sequence using rough visuals and temporary audio.

Record aspect ratio, safe areas, shot duration, and visual continuity requirements.

## 4. Motion principles

Use timing, spacing, easing, anticipation, follow-through, staging, and hierarchy to communicate intent. Motion should clarify relationships, guide attention, convey feedback, or support storytelling. Avoid unnecessary movement, inconsistent easing, excessive bounce, and transitions that delay comprehension.

For interface motion, respect interaction state and reduced-motion preferences. Essential information must remain available without animation.

## 5. Animation production

For 2D animation, plan layers, rigs, key poses, in-betweens, timing, and compositing. For 3D animation, verify rig integrity, deformation, camera, lighting, render settings, and scene dependencies. Maintain character, object, scale, and environment continuity across shots.

Do not describe a still image as an animation or a storyboard as a finished video.

## 6. Editing and compositing

Organize footage and assets, establish a coherent sequence, maintain screen direction and continuity, and use transitions purposefully. Check titles, lower thirds, captions, color correction, visual effects, and compositing edges. Avoid unintended black frames, flashes, mismatched shots, clipped text, and inconsistent color.

## 7. Audio

Use clear, balanced dialogue or narration. Mix music and effects so they support rather than obscure speech. Check loudness, clipping, noise, sync, channel layout, and fade behavior. Verify rights and permitted use for music, voice, samples, and sound effects.

Provide captions or transcripts when appropriate. Do not use copyrighted music or third-party recordings without appropriate rights.

## 8. Export and delivery

Match export settings to the destination: codec, container, resolution, frame rate, bitrate, audio codec, aspect ratio, and file size. Common formats include MP4, MOV, WebM, GIF, and image sequences, depending on platform and use.

Check the rendered output from beginning to end. Verify playback, synchronization, compression artifacts, text readability, safe areas, and platform compatibility. Preserve the editable project and linked media when requested.

## 9. Versioning and review

Use clear version names and keep source assets organized. Collect timestamped feedback where useful. Distinguish corrections from creative changes and scope additions. Preserve approved sequences and perform a full or targeted review after changes based on impact.

## Quality checks

- Message, story, and call to action.
- Timing, pacing, continuity, and transitions.
- Character/object consistency and motion quality.
- Typography, captions, safe areas, and readability.
- Audio clarity, levels, synchronization, and rights.
- Color, exposure, compositing, and render artifacts.
- Export settings, playback, file integrity, and target compatibility.
- Source project and media dependencies.

## Deliverables

Provide final video/animation exports, editable project files when requested, source media and license notes, captions/transcript, export specifications, and a concise handoff listing duration, frame rate, resolution, format, and known limitations.

## Definition of done

The complete rendered work communicates the approved message, motion and audio are coherent, exports match destination requirements, rights and accessibility needs are addressed, and the final output has been reviewed in playback.
