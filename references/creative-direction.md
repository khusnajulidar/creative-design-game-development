# Creative Direction

## Purpose

Creative direction translates a goal into a coherent creative intention and guides the decisions that shape the final experience. It owns the overall "why", "for whom", "what should be felt or understood", and "how the parts fit together". Specialist disciplines own detailed execution.

## 1. Discovery

Establish:
- The problem, opportunity, or message.
- Primary and secondary audiences, including their context and needs.
- Desired action, understanding, emotion, or outcome.
- Medium, platform, distribution, and use environment.
- Brand, cultural, legal, accessibility, and technical constraints.
- Existing assets, references, prior decisions, and what must not change.
- Budget, schedule, production capacity, and approval process where known.

Separate confirmed facts from assumptions. Ask focused questions only where missing information materially changes the direction. Otherwise record assumptions and proceed.

## 2. Creative strategy

Convert discovery into a concise strategic foundation:
- **Objective:** the result the work must achieve.
- **Audience insight:** a relevant need, behavior, or context.
- **Core message:** the one idea the audience should retain.
- **Value proposition:** why the work matters to that audience.
- **Creative concept:** the organizing idea that makes the work distinctive.
- **Tone and personality:** how the work should communicate.
- **Success criteria:** observable evidence that the direction is effective.

Avoid vague objectives such as "make it attractive". Translate them into measurable or reviewable criteria.

## 3. Concept development

Generate concepts that solve the brief, not merely decorate it. Evaluate each against:
- Relevance to objective and audience.
- Clarity and memorability.
- Distinctiveness and brand fit.
- Emotional and experiential coherence.
- Feasibility, production effort, and platform suitability.
- Accessibility and cultural appropriateness.
- Rights and source constraints.

Present a recommended direction with rationale. Offer multiple routes only when comparison is useful or requested. Do not create superficial variants that differ only in minor decoration.

## 4. Reference handling and originality

Classify references as:
- **Required:** must be followed closely.
- **Structural:** informs layout, flow, or behavior.
- **Stylistic:** informs mood, palette, or visual language.
- **Inspirational:** a starting point, not a specification.

Record what must be retained and what may be adapted. Respect exact user-provided assets when requested. Do not replace a real photograph, logo, or approved illustration with an AI-generated approximation unless the user authorizes that change.

For original exploration, derive a direction from the brief rather than copying a reference. Do not reproduce protected expression or imply affiliation with another brand.

## 5. Experience and storytelling

Consider the whole audience journey:
1. First contact and expectation.
2. Orientation and understanding.
3. Main interaction or content.
4. Feedback, emotion, and progression.
5. Completion and next action.

Ensure the visual language, content, interaction, pacing, and feedback reinforce the same purpose. Remove elements that compete with the primary message or task.

## 6. Direction system

Document the direction at a level useful to the production team:
- Mood, tone, and key attributes.
- Visual principles and hierarchy.
- Color and typography intent.
- Image, illustration, icon, and material direction.
- Composition and spatial principles.
- Motion, sound, and interaction principles where relevant.
- Content voice and terminology.
- Examples of acceptable and unacceptable treatments.
- Platform and accessibility requirements.

A moodboard alone is not a complete specification when production requires exact dimensions, assets, behavior, or technical details.

## 7. Feasibility and collaboration

Consult relevant specialists early. Check whether the concept can be produced, integrated, maintained, and delivered within the actual constraints. Identify dependencies such as fonts, licenses, media, data, APIs, device capability, localization, or specialist software.

When a concept is not feasible as proposed, explain why and recommend the closest practical solution that preserves the core intent.

## 8. Review and revision control

Review against the brief and agreed criteria, not personal taste alone. Consolidate feedback and distinguish:
- Defects or unmet requirements.
- Usability or communication problems.
- Optional improvements.
- New requests outside the approved scope.

Make focused revisions. Preserve approved decisions unless a defect or newly authorized change requires reconsideration. Record consequential changes and their effects on schedule, cost, assets, and downstream work.

## 9. Deliverables

Depending on the project, provide:
- Creative brief or strategy.
- Concept rationale and selected direction.
- Moodboard or visual references with source notes.
- Experience or storytelling outline.
- Creative/visual direction specification.
- Asset and production requirements.
- Review notes and approval status.
- Handoff to specialist disciplines.

## Definition of done

The creative direction is ready when the objective and audience are clear, the concept is approved or explicitly provisional, the key creative principles are documented, references and constraints are understood, feasibility has been considered, and downstream teams can execute without inventing the central direction.
